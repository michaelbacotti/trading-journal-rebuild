What the tape is saying

Monday August 24 opens as the first trading day after a broadly dovish Jackson Hole symposium. The Federal Reserve Chair's keynote — delivered Friday afternoon — emphasized labor market caution and global risks, consistent with a 25 basis point rate cut at the September 17-18 FOMC meeting. Markets closed Friday near session highs following the speech, and the pre-event defensive positioning has been partially vindicated by the Fed's dovish communication.

SPY at $765.72 is up 3.63% over 20 trading days — a steady, sustained advance — but down 1.37% over the past five trading sessions. The 5-day decline is a function of pre-event de-risking into Jackson Hole and has reversed sharply. The critical breadth signal has snapped back to 100% of SPX components above their 50-day moving average after Friday's brief dip to 75%. The VIX at 15.91 has ticked up modestly from last Monday's 15.16 close — a 5% increase in absolute terms. This is a small move that reflects the Jackson Hole event risk rather than a meaningful regime shift. The VIX3M at 18.50 produces a term ratio of 0.860 — backwardation, meaning front-month implied volatility is priced below the 3-month. This remains the classic bull-market vol structure: any volatility event would expand front-month IV faster than back-month. VIX below 16 remains historically compressed.

The yield curve (2s10s at 0 basis points) is flat but no longer inverted. The curve is at the boundary between normal and inverted. Financials (XLF +2.08% on the 20-day) reflect the improving backdrop as the curve normalizes from inverted to flat. The flat curve is neutral-to-constructive for bank stocks and removes the worst of the margin compression that existed through 2023-2025. The curve's stability post-Jackson Hole is a baseline of macro normalcy.

The dollar (DXY at 27.90) has weakened approximately 2.38% over the past 20 trading days. Dollar weakness is normally a bullish macro catalyst for equities — cheaper dollar supports multinationals, eases financial conditions, and can be supportive for commodities. However, in the current environment of strong energy prices and geopolitical risk, dollar weakness combined with crude strength warrants monitoring as a potential stagflation signal rather than a simple growth catalyst.
