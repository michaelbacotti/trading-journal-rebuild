Market Outlook — Monday, August 10, 2026: Bull Trend Intact, CPI Tops the Week, Mega-Cap Tech Leads

SPY $773.26, QQQ $723.03, SPX ~7,758. VIX 15.47, term ratio 0.753 backwardated. 100% breadth above 50d MA. XLK +7.20% 5d leads; XLE -3.44% 5d lags. CPI Aug 11 is the week's macro pivot.

What the tape is saying

The broad market is in a grinding bull phase heading into the new week. SPX closed Friday at approximately 7,758 — within striking distance of all-time highs — after a 5-day run that added over 3.5% to SPY. The market pulled back mildly from Wednesday's intraday peak (SPX ~7,737) to close Friday at 7,757.64, a contained and orderly 0.35% pullback that completed a strong week. SPY closed at $773.26, sitting comfortably above both its 50-day moving average ($746.61) and its 200-day moving average ($700.13) — the hallmark configuration of a sustained uptrend.

The most important single fact in this week's tape: 100% of SPX components are above their 50-day moving average going into Monday's open. This is not a fragile, narrow mega-cap rally. It is broad participation — the kind of market structure that extends trend longevity rather than shortening it. When breadth is at maximum, the odds of a sudden, disorderly reversal decrease; corrections tend to be shallow and brief.

QQQ is the complexity in the picture. The technology-heavy index closed at $723.03 — up a strong +5.09% over the past 5 days but down -0.34% over the past 20 days. The 5-day momentum is the more recent and more relevant read, and it confirms that the technology-heavy complex has fully recovered from the July AI-capex-driven selloff. The 20-day lag is a signal worth monitoring: it means QQQ spent the better part of a month net flat while SPY made new highs, and the breadth of the recovery still needs to be confirmed. The current 5-day bounce is constructive, but the 20-day relative picture has not yet normalized.

IWM is the bright spot on the relative-performance side. The Russell 2000 ETF is +3.56% over 5 days and +1.88% over 20 days, cleanly above both its 50-day and 200-day moving averages. Small-cap participation is the kind of tape confirmation that broadens the bull market beyond mega-cap leadership and is consistent with a fully established risk-on environment.

Bullish factors

  1. Market structure confirms the trend. SPY is above its 50-day and 200-day moving averages, with 5-day momentum (+3.51%) accelerating relative to 20-day momentum (+2.43%). This pattern — momentum acceleration over the short term within an established trend — is the tape pattern most associated with sustained directional moves. The 50-day ($746.61) and 200-day ($700.13) moving averages are well below spot, providing a deep layer of structural support.
  2. Breadth is at maximum. 100% of SPX names above their 50-day moving average is an extraordinary breadth reading. It means the rally is not dependent on a handful of mega-cap names carrying the index. Broad participation distributes risk across the market, which reduces the probability of a sudden, leader-driven reversal. The same reading held on Friday's close, confirming it is the operative state of the tape.
  3. The VIX term structure is backwardated. VIX at 15.47 versus VIX3M at 20.54 produces a term ratio of 0.753 — front-month implied volatility at a discount to the 3-month strip. Backwardation is the standard structural signal that option hedgers are net short near-term protection. It means the market is comfortable with its risk posture and has not yet moved to fear-based hedging. Historically, a backwardated VIX term structure in a rising market is a constructive backdrop for continued equity performance.
  4. XLK Technology is leading on the 5-day window. XLK +7.20% over the past 5 days is the strongest sector read of the week. Mega-cap earnings reframed the AI narrative from a chip-supplier capex debate to a hyperscaler revenue conversion story. The 5-day leadership is the live market's vote on which side of the AI debate it is on, and that vote is decisive.
  5. QQQ is recovering. QQQ closed at $723.03, +5.09% over 5 days, above its 50-day MA ($714.31) and significantly above its 200-day MA ($646.70). The 20-day is still slightly negative, but the 5-day momentum has materially accelerated. QQQ has rejoined the broader uptrend; the remaining normalization is in the 20-day relative-performance comparison.
  6. The yield curve is no longer inverted. The 2s10s spread at 0 basis points means the curve is flat, not inverted. This is the first time in this cycle that the curve has been flat or positive. Historically, curve inversion has preceded economic contraction signals by 6 to 18 months. Its absence removes one of the primary macro headwinds that have pressured cyclical sectors and small-caps throughout 2025 and 2026.
  7. IWM is participating. The Russell 2000 ETF is +3.56% over 5 days and +1.88% over 20 days, with both timeframes cleanly above the 50-day MA ($293.71). Small-cap participation is the kind of tape confirmation that broadens the bull market beyond mega-cap leadership. IWM's relative participation is one of the most constructive cross-asset signals in the current tape.
  8. No event risk on Monday. The absence of major macroeconomic releases or high-profile earnings on the open of the week means the tape can continue to operate on its own technical and fundamental logic without counterparty risk from headline-driven volatility events. An event-free Monday is historically a setup for trend continuation, particularly when breadth is at maximum.

Bearish factors

  1. QQQ's 20-day underperformance. QQQ is -0.34% over 20 days while SPY is +2.43% over the same period. The 5-day momentum (+5.09%) is the more recent read and is encouraging, but the 20-day lag signals that QQQ spent the better part of a month net flat while SPY made new highs. If this divergence deepens — QQQ breaking below its 50-day MA while SPY holds — it would be an early warning of leadership narrowing. A bull market supported by 4 to 5 mega-cap names is more fragile than one supported by broad sector participation.
  2. QQQ's elevated realized volatility. HV 20d at 26.09% is more than 11 percentage points above SPY's HV 20d of 14.35%. This is not inherently bearish — QQQ has structurally higher volatility — but the current gap is at the wider end of the historical range. High realized volatility without a corresponding directional acceleration typically means the market is choppy. The 5-day momentum is the bullish counter; the 26.09% HV is the risk.
  3. XLE Energy is the worst 5-day performer. XLE is -3.44% over the past 5 days after leading on the 20-day window (+4.39% vs SPY). The pattern is classic profit-taking in a bull trend, but the magnitude of the 5-day reversal is notable. WTI crude near $81 is the key level: a hold above $78 keeps the broader narrative intact; a break below $78 would signal that the commodity cycle is rolling over.
  4. XLU Utilities lagging on the 20-day. XLU is -3.96% over 20 days — the worst 20-day performance by a wide margin. Defensive lag is normal in a bull market, but the magnitude of XLU's 20-day weakness reflects rate sensitivity as the 10-year yield has stabilized in the upper portion of its recent range. If 10-year yields break higher, XLU is the most direct casualty.
  5. Breadth at 100% is statistically extreme. The current reading is extraordinary and historically mean-reverts. A 100% reading does not mean breadth will deteriorate immediately — it can persist for weeks in a strong trend — but the closer the reading is to maximum, the more important it is to monitor the rate of change. A drop from 100% to 80% in a single week would be an early warning of trend stress.
  6. DXY is below its 50-day MA. The dollar at 28.07 sits below its 50-day MA (28.23) with a 20-day return of -1.13%. A weaker dollar has been a tailwind for risk assets and multinational earnings, but it is also a structural inflation pressure. If the dollar breaks its 50-day MA to the downside, the inflation read becomes more complex.
  7. Put/call ratio at 0.85 is neutral-to-bullish but not extreme. A very low put/call ratio (below 0.7) historically reflects stretched bullish positioning. The current 0.85 reading is not extreme, but it is the kind of value that occasionally precedes short-term consolidation when combined with maximum breadth.
  8. Tomorrow's CPI print is the dominant near-term risk. A hot print (headline above +3.1% YoY, core above +3.3% YoY) would push out Fed rate-cut expectations and pressure equity multiples. The Goldilocks narrative that has supported the rally depends on inflation continuing to drift benignly. The market is positioned for stability; a surprise in either direction would move the tape.

CPI setup

The primary macro event of the week is the July CPI release on Tuesday, August 11. Consensus expects headline +3.1% YoY and core +3.3% YoY. The inflation print is the single most consequential data release in the calendar for the next five days and will set the tone for the rate-cut expectations that have supported multiple expansion across the equity tape.

The setup is asymmetric. The market has been trading on a Goldilocks narrative — benign inflation gliding lower while growth remains steady. SPY has accelerated over the past 5 days (+3.51%) and breadth is at 100%, both of which are consistent with the market pricing stability into the print. A print at or below consensus (headline +3.1% or lower, core +3.3% or lower) would support the narrative and likely extend the rally. A hot print (headline +3.2% or higher, core +3.4% or higher) would disrupt the narrative and push out rate-cut expectations, which would pressure equity multiples.

The secondary inflation read is the July PPI release on Wednesday, August 12. Markets will focus on the services component as a leading indicator for CPI services. A weak PPI services print would reinforce the CPI narrative; a strong PPI services print would compound the CPI risk into a back-to-back data shock.

The macro setup going into CPI is favorable for the bull case but with low tolerance for upside surprises. The compressed volatility (VIX 15.47) and the elevated breadth (100%) are both indicators that the market has positioned for stability, and a hot print would force a repricing of that positioning.

Risks to this outlook

  • CPI surprise on Aug 11 is the primary near-term risk. A hot print would disrupt the Goldilocks narrative and push out Fed rate-cut expectations. Rate-cut expectations are currently supportive of equity multiples; repricing them higher would be a headwind. The market is positioned for stability and has limited tolerance for upside surprises.
  • NVDA earnings on Aug 19 are a known binary event. Given XLK's strong +7.20% 5-day performance, some of the move may be positioning ahead of the print. A clean beat extends the sector's leadership; a miss reverses it quickly. Size any technology exposure accordingly heading into that date.
  • VIX spike is the dominant tail risk. VIX at 15.47 leaves meaningful room for expansion. A geopolitical event — Strait of Hormuz tension, an Iran nuclear escalation, or a surprise from a major central bank — could cause a rapid re-pricing of near-term vol. A VIX spike to 20+ would represent a significant vol event that could unwind short-vol positions rapidly even if the directional outcome is a temporary pullback.
  • QQQ 20-day underperformance deepening is the secondary structural risk. QQQ -0.34% over 20 days while SPY +2.43% is the signal. If QQQ breaks below its 50-day MA while SPY holds, the bull market is narrowing around mega-cap tech and becomes more fragile. This is the most important technical watch item for the coming week.
  • Breadth deterioration from 100% is a structural watch item. The current 100% reading is extraordinary and historically mean-reverts. A drop from 100% to 80% in a single week would be an early warning of trend stress — not a reason to exit, but a reason to tighten risk management and reduce concentration.
  • Oil price risk is asymmetric. Any geopolitical disruption in a Strait of Hormuz scenario, an OPEC+ production surprise, or an Iran-related escalation could spike crude and reintroduce inflation concerns. The current benign oil backdrop near $81 is a pillar of the Goldilocks narrative. It is a condition, not a certainty.
  • Treasury yield risk is a medium-term concern. The 10-year yield stabilizing in the upper portion of its recent range is the proximate cause of XLU's 20-day weakness. A breakout higher in 10-year yields would pressure rate-sensitive sectors (utilities, REITs, growth) and could compress equity multiples broadly.
